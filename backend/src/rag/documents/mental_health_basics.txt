MENTAL HEALTH BASICS

Mental health refers to emotional, psychological, and social well-being. It affects how people think, feel, behave, handle stress, relate to others, and make decisions.

STRESS

Stress is a normal response to challenging or demanding situations. Common sources include academic workload, work responsibilities, financial difficulties, relationship problems, major life changes, and uncertainty.

Common signs of stress may include difficulty concentrating, irritability, feeling overwhelmed, changes in sleep, fatigue, headaches, muscle tension, and changes in appetite.

Helpful coping strategies may include breaking large tasks into smaller steps, maintaining a regular sleep schedule, taking short breaks, physical activity, relaxation or breathing exercises, maintaining social connections, and talking with someone trusted.

ANXIETY

Anxiety involves feelings of worry, fear, nervousness, or apprehension. Occasional anxiety can be a normal response to stressful situations.

When anxiety becomes persistent, excessive, difficult to control, or interferes significantly with daily activities, school, work, relationships, or sleep, professional mental-health support may be appropriate.

DEPRESSION

Depression is more than temporary sadness. It can involve persistent low mood or loss of interest or pleasure, along with changes in sleep, appetite, energy, concentration, feelings of worthlessness, or hopelessness.

A mental-health professional should assess persistent or severe symptoms. A chatbot should not diagnose depression.

SELF-CARE

Basic self-care can support mental well-being. Examples include adequate sleep, regular physical activity, balanced nutrition, social connection, relaxation activities, setting realistic goals, taking breaks, and asking for help when needed.

PROFESSIONAL SUPPORT

People experiencing persistent or severe emotional difficulties may benefit from speaking with a qualified counselor, psychologist, psychiatrist, doctor, or other appropriate mental-health professional.

A mental-health chatbot provides general information and supportive conversation. It should not replace professional assessment, diagnosis, treatment, or emergency services.

CRISIS AND IMMEDIATE DANGER

If someone is in immediate danger, has seriously harmed themselves, has an immediate intention to harm themselves or another person, or cannot keep themselves safe, they should seek immediate help from local emergency services, a nearby healthcare facility, or a trusted person who can stay with them.

The chatbot should respond calmly, encourage immediate human support, and avoid providing instructions for self-harm or harm to others.
